# Changelog

Notable changes to OpenFlyover. The desktop installers of each version are on the
[Releases](https://github.com/YannickRiou/openflyover/releases) page (`docs/installers.md`).

## 4.5.0 (October 2026)

- **Dependencies of the app updated**, security fixes included: the list is in the commits since 4.4.1.
- **The camera smooths out its own jolts**, with nothing to press or set (existing films may change a little): « Lissage de la caméra » at 0 or 0.5 s no longer lets the camera whip round the hairpins of a trail (the heading is always averaged over at least a second of flight); a speed portion at ×4 no longer makes « Oiseau » swing 4 times as fast through the bends; between two camera keys, the camera no longer goes through a ridge, nor jumps as it reaches a key; the turn to a photo pin never takes the marker out of the frame any more; and when sharper terrain tiles arrive while the film plays, the camera climbs to its new height over a moment instead of hopping.
- **A camera that is nicer to watch: « Espace devant » and « Inclinaison dans les virages »**: two sliders in « Survol › Caméra », with gentle defaults that old projects get too (their films change a little; both at 0 give the old framing back). « Espace devant » (15 % by default, up to 40 %) puts the marker a little behind the middle of the picture, on the side it comes from, so there is room on the side it is heading to; it comes back to the middle when the marker stops. « Inclinaison dans les virages » (3° by default, up to 10°) tilts the horizon gently into a bend and levels it on straight stretches and at stops. The opening and closing shots, the keyframes and the high views level the horizon again smoothly, and a keyframe added by hand never keeps the tilt. Neither acts in « Orbite autour du marqueur », the tilt not in « Vue du dessus ». The preview, the video export and the poster show the same picture. « Nouveautés » presents it.
- **A marker that walks and takes the light**: the figurine of an animated marker walks at the marker's pace (quicker when the film speeds up, standing still at a stop or a night), faces the way the track goes on screen without flicker on a switchback and turns round smoothly instead of flipping, and the text under the marker is never mirrored. Every marker also takes a little of the light of the scene: warm at sunrise and sunset, slightly bluish and softer at night, always readable. Nothing to set, projects included.
- **Records that make sense for a flight, a switch and « Recalculer les records »**: the records of the outing no longer look for the steepest climb, the fastest descent, the fastest and slowest kilometre or the longest climb on the stretches flown of a track (a slope means nothing in a paraglider; a track flown from start to end has none of them); the highest and lowest points, the heart rate and the power stay. A flight gets two records of its own: « Meilleur thermique » (the best climb rate held about 25 s, at least +1 m/s, shown « +3,2 m/s », with an automatic stop like the steepest climb) and « Plus haut au-dessus du sol » (a label only; the height is measured when the track is imported, so a track imported before this version has it only once imported again). « Carte › Repères et points d'intérêt » has a « Records de la sortie » switch (on by default) that hides or shows all the records, labels and automatic stops, without deleting anything, and « Recalculer les records », which reads the tracks again and brings the automatic stops the film wrote out for records in line (it removes those of records that no longer exist, adds the missing ones, never touches the stops you made) in one undo step, with a message saying what changed and « Annuler ». « Nouveautés » presents it.
- **Flights are easier to read** (paragliding, kite jumps, cable cars: the stretches drawn in the air): a translucent **curtain** falls from the line down to the relief, fading towards the ground, so you see how high the flight goes; where the flight passes over itself (the turns of a thermal) it stops on the pass below, so the turns read as a stack of bands, and « Longueur du rideau » (20 m to 1000 m, « Jusqu'au sol » by default) makes it shorter; a soft **shadow** of the flight lies on the relief right under it; and the line is **thicker when it comes closer to the camera and thinner farther away** (from ×0.6 to ×1.6 of the width you set, eased in and out at the ends of the flight, automatic). The curtain and the shadow each have a switch in a new « Vol » group of the « Trace » section (shown only when a loaded track has a flight, on by default). « Colorer selon » also gets **« Vitesse verticale »** for tracks with times: yellow where you climb, purple where you sink, the middle of the legend being 0 m/s, so thermals show at a glance. « Nouveautés » presents it.
- **« Focale »: choose the lens of the film**: a new row in « Survol › Caméra » sets the focal length of the whole film, from 18 mm (wide angle: more space around) to 135 mm (telephoto: the mountains look closer and more imposing), in every camera style. The camera backs off or comes closer with the lens, so the marker keeps the same size on screen and only the perspective changes, like swapping lenses; the opening and closing shots, the overviews and the group view of « En parallèle » still frame the whole track (a wide angle leaves them where they were, so you see more land around). 26 mm is the image of the earlier versions: films do not change until the slider is moved, and projects saved before open at 26 mm. The presets set 26 mm; another focal length shows the « Personnalisé » tile. The preview and the export use the same lens, and « Profondeur de champ » keeps the same depth sharp in metres whatever the lens (a telephoto blurs the far mountains more, as a real one). « Nouveautés » presents it.

## 4.4.1 (October 2026)

- **The marker no longer blinks behind the relief**: it was switched off whole as soon as the computed sight line touched the relief, and back on as soon as it cleared it, so a line grazing a crest (a low view, or a higher one over small mountains) made it blink, and it showed whole over a summit that was really in front of it. The relief now hides the marker like a real object, from the image itself: a crest in front of it covers it where it passes, a little at first, then all of it, without blinking, and the slope just under it (150 m) never cuts it. The same for the markers of « En parallèle ». Its place and size on screen do not change; the 3D labels still disappear whole behind the relief.

## 4.4.0 (October 2026)

- **« Clarté des nuits »: nights you can see**: at night the relief was lit only by a very faint sky light, so a film crossing a night (or set at a night hour) often showed almost nothing. A new slider in « Lumière › Atmosphère » lights the relief and the clouds at night, like moonlight, from 0 IL (as before, the default: films do not change) to +4 IL; it has no effect while the sun is up and fades in with dusk. The sky, the stars and the daytime image stay as they were. « Exposition » moved from « Atmosphère » to « Couleurs », just above contrast and saturation (same setting, same keys on the timeline).
- **Two settings next to their neighbours**: « Durée des changements de vitesse » moved from the bottom of « Survol › Durée et rythme » to « Survol › Caméra », right under « Fin en douceur » (it is shown in every camera style, « Libre » too). « Tige et ombre au sol » left « Plus d'options » of « Trace › Marqueur » for the line right under « Taille du marqueur ». Same settings, same values: films do not change.
- **Several logos**: « Habillage › Météo, logo et texte » now has « Ajouter un logo » (up to four logos, for example the club's at the bottom right and a sponsor's at the top left). Each logo has its own switch, image, position (the 3 × 3 frame or « Position libre »), « Taille » and « Opacité »; « Supprimer ce logo » takes an added logo away (the first one stays, with « Retirer » for its image). Logos in the same corner are stacked one under the other like the other elements, in the preview and in the video export. In the project the single `overlay.logo` became the list `overlay.logos`: projects and presets saved before open with their logo as the first one, so their films do not change (a project saved with this version does not keep its logos in an older one). Presets still keep the project's own logo images. « Nouveautés » presents it.
- **Day cards choose their colours, fonts and shadow**: « Cartes de jour » (Lumière › « Jours et nuits », and the inspector of a day card block) now has « Couleurs et polices », like the opening and closing cards: accent, text, panel (or « Fond du texte », the shadow or outline of the text, in a style without panel) and fonts, the same for every day, « Comme le reste de l'habillage » to go back. Until changed the cards keep the look of the overlay, so projects saved before open with the same film.

## 4.3.0 (October 2026)

- **« Qualité de l'aperçu »** (Réglages › Aperçu 3D): « Auto », « Légère » or « Complète ». A light preview draws at a pixel ratio of 1.5 at most, 1 while the film plays, and its clouds at half resolution, averaged over 16 still frames instead of 32. « Auto » (the default) judges the device by what it does, not by its hardware: when the preview runs below 30 frames per second during playback, it stays light for the session (the panel says so). Exported films do not change: the export sets its own pixel ratio and cloud quality. « Diagnostic » lists the values in use. On a phone or tablet, the lower pixel ratio while playing now also holds after a track finishes loading.
- **Landmark settings answer at once on long tracks**: each change of « Repères et points d'intérêt » (a kind, the distance, the density, « Toujours », « Masquer »), a recolour, and each OpenStreetMap answer measured again every landmark against every point of every track. A landmark is now measured once per track; the settings only filter again. The landmarks shown are the same.
- **Lighter at rest and on long projects**: the home screen no longer creates the 3D view (a laptop's dedicated graphics card stays asleep until a track is opened); a hidden tab pauses the film, its music and video clips with it, and the status bar stops its once-a-second readout; the autosave no longer encodes every point of every track after each change; the water, forest and glacier requests of a closed project, a removed track or a layer switched off are cancelled instead of holding the OpenStreetMap queue; changing a track's colour or name no longer re-places the forests nor rebuilds the water, the glaciers and the 3D line. Exported films do not change.
- **The user guide in every language of the app**: « Premiers pas » on the home screen opens the guide in the language of the interface (English, French, Spanish, Italian, German), each page quoting the labels as that interface shows them, with links to the other languages at the top. The CI checks that every translation keeps the sections, anchors and pictures of the English guide.
- **The rest of the project follows « Outils de la trace » too**: Ctrl+Z after a tool put the stops and keys of the film back at their former distances, on the new points (a stop moved before « Rogner le début » of 5 km came back 5 km off); applying a tool or its « Annuler » now clears the undo history, as opening a project does (the tool's « Annuler » stays). The glaciers fetched for the former points no longer stay on screen while those of the new ones load, or when that request fails. The GPX waypoints set on a stretch removed by « Rogner » or « Retirer une section » (within 200 m of it) go with it, from the 3D labels and from « Exporter la trace en GPX »; the waypoints away from the track stay.

## 4.2.1 (October 2026)

- **The weather follows « Outils de la trace »**: after « Rogner la fin », « Retirer une section », « Supprimer les pics GPS », « Retirer les pauses », « Simplifier » or « Inverser le sens » (tools that keep the start time) the weather of the outing, the clouds, the wind and the snow line were still those fetched for the former points; they are fetched again for the new ones, as after « Décaler l'heure ». The stages « À la suite » follow too. The tools already changed the track loaded in the project itself (the 3D line, the figures, the film and the landmarks follow « Appliquer », « Annuler » puts it back): there is no need to export the GPX and open it again, « Exporter la trace en GPX » is only for using the clean track elsewhere.
- **« Durée du survol » says what the film is made of**: with a 24 h outing that crossed a night, 25 s typed gave a « Survol » block of 33 s because the night shown as a pause (dusk 3 s + night 2 s + dawn 3 s) is added to the flyover, whatever « Garder la durée du survol ». The panel now prints the film length as its main figure and, under it, « Ouverture 5 s + survol 33 s + clôture 6 s. Au-delà des 25 s réglées, le survol compte : 1 nuit (8 s). », and the sentences under the field say whether the stops and slow-motion are taken from the duration (« Garder » ticked) or added to it (unticked). No film changes length.
- **« × temps réel » is now « × votre allure réelle »**: the speed-up was computed against the recorded time of the file (24 h 33 for a day with a lunch and a bivouac), so 100 km/h on a 16.5 km walk at 2 km/h read × 149. It now counts the time actually spent moving (the stops of « Retirer les pauses », the recording gaps of over 10 minutes, the gaps between segments and the nights left out; the duration typed in « Corriger les chiffres » is kept; the stages of « À la suite » add up), so it reads × 50. The panel writes the marker's speed in the film, the factor and the pace you really kept (« vous avanciez à 2,0 km/h : 8 h 15 en mouvement »), on both faces of « Régler par ». The stored duration is unchanged: old projects open with the same films.
- **The render queue renders each film at the format ticked for it**: a project made as a Reel (9:16) and queued with 16:9 ticked in « Plusieurs formats » came out as a 16:9 picture holding a 9:16 composition (the frame and the size the overlay's free positions refer to were those of the project), and the same the other way round. Each film of an item is now composed for its own format, as if the project had been made in it (the poster keeps the project's own); the project saved and the one open are untouched.
- **Removing the last track closes the project**: the ✕ of the last track's card now does what « Fermer le projet » does (the same « Fermer sans enregistrer ? » question when changes are saved nowhere, then an empty project with the default settings), instead of leaving an empty but « Modifié » project that made quitting ask to save it. « Annuler » in the message puts the track and the project back.
- **« Ouvrir » asks what to do with a GPX or FIT file**: when a project that already holds tracks is open, opening a track file (the folder icon, Ctrl+O or a drop on the window) asks « Que faire de ce fichier ? » with « Ajouter la trace à ce projet » (as before), « Commencer un nouveau projet » (closes the project like « Nouveau projet », asking « Fermer sans enregistrer ? » if needed) and « Annuler ». One question for several files; none for an empty project or a project file; the « Ajouter » button and the Strava import still add straight away.
- **Photos on the terrain**: each photo or video of the timeline now stands on the relief as a 3D pin (a post with its framed thumbnail, facing the view, a little smaller far away) at the place where it was taken: its EXIF GPS position when the file has one (within 2 km of the track and 500 m of where the marker is when it appears), otherwise where the marker is when it appears. About 15 seconds before the photo appears the camera turns towards the pin without moving, the pin lights up while the photo is on screen, and the camera turns back afterwards; a camera key set by hand wins over the turn, and the opening and closing shots ease it in and out. Pins close to each other are stacked on taller posts instead of hiding each other and give way to the 3D labels. The video export shows the same pins and the same camera turn as the preview (the pin keeps its share of the frame height at every resolution); the poster does not show the pins. Setting « Habillage › Étiquettes 3D › Photos sur le terrain », on for a new project; projects saved before open with it off, so their films do not change. « Nouveautés » presents it.
## 4.2.0 (October 2026)

- **Records of the outing**: « Carte › Repères et points d'intérêt » now lists the feats of the track: the steepest climb (« 18 % sur 400 m »), the fastest descent (from 8 km/h), the highest point (unless it is a summit already listed) and the lowest one, the fastest and slowest kilometre (timed tracks), the longest climb (from two climbs) and the peak heart rate and power. Computed from the points of the track only (smoothed elevations, moving time, GPS jumps over 150 km/h ignored), none under 1 km, and the figures corrected by hand do not change them. Each record is a landmark like the others (trophy label in the 3D view, Auto / Toujours / Jamais, ranking, « Arrêt »), shown by default, « Arrêts automatiques » stops at the steepest climb, the highest point and the fastest descent (the other records stay labels; « Arrêt » adds a stop to any) with a card showing the record and its value; films saved with the pauses of the former pacing keep their pauses. The trophy is also offered as a pictogram of the points of interest. The OpenStreetMap credit no longer shows for records alone. « Nouveautés » presents them.
- **« Outils de la trace »**: one line « Outils de la trace… » on each track's card of the « Trace » tab opens a window of its own (the editing panels stay about the film) with seven tools that retouch the loaded track (never the file): the profile and the plan with the part concerned highlighted at the top, the list of tools on the left, the tool's settings and the distance, D+, duration and number of points before → after on the right. « Appliquer » makes one step with « Annuler » in the message and in the window; a tool that changes nothing says so. On a phone the window is full screen.
  - « Rogner le début et la fin » (drag on the profile or the two sliders), « Retirer une section » (« Relier en ligne droite » or « Couper en deux morceaux »).
  - « Supprimer les pics GPS » (a point jumped at least 100 m and faster than 4 times the median speed, adjustable; the window says how many points will go), « Retirer les pauses » (the track stays within 15 m for 2 minutes by default, listed with where and how long; the later times move back, so the duration and average speed no longer count them).
  - « Simplifier » (Douglas–Peucker, 2 m by default, points before → after, a warning above 8 m), « Inverser le sens » (times stay increasing) and « Décaler l'heure » (hours and minutes, or a new start; the weather and the snow follow).
  - « Exporter la trace en GPX »: a clean GPX 1.1 of the track as it is now, with heart rate, cadence, power and temperature.
  - What the film anchors along the track (stops, camera and parameter keys, marker keys, speed portions) follows the places it was set on, on a single track and on the stages « À la suite »: after a trim or a cut, a reversal or the pauses, a stop stays on the same spot. Items on a removed stretch are deleted and the window counts them.
  - A track without times greys out the tools that need them, with the reason. Figures corrected by hand that a tool makes wrong are cleared (the window says how many before « Appliquer »). Older projects open unchanged (a modified track is stored like any track).
- **« Bilan »: one film of a season or a year of outings**: « Trace › Plusieurs traces » has a fourth mode, next to « La première », « À la suite » and « En parallèle ». The tracks already imported (several files at once, or the Strava activities of a period) draw themselves one after the other, in date order, over a high fixed view of their region (the « Libre » framing of the region, a little tilted); the ones already drawn stay in a dimmer tone, and a date stamp in the bottom-left corner gives the date and the name of the outing being drawn. Each outing takes the same time: the flyover duration shared between them, at least 2 s each.
  - Tracks more than 50 km from each other make separate regions, one chapter each: its own high view, a fade to black from one region to the next (a cut between two outings of one region) and a card at the start of each region (« Région 2 sur 3 », outings, distance, D+, months), shown or hidden by « Une carte au début de chaque région ».
  - The counters (Habillage › Compteurs) add up the outings drawn so far: the new « Sorties », distance, D+ and time. The closing card shows the totals of the year by default (outings, distance, D+, time, highest point; « Sorties » is a new figure of the cards, offered for a Bilan only).
  - The film does of its own: the camera stays on the region (the opening and closing shots only add their seconds), the lines always draw themselves one by one, the order is the dates' (an outing without a date comes after the others of its region), and no landmarks, water, forests, glaciers, start and finish pins or weather per outing are shown (no request per outing). Old projects open unchanged (`race.bilan` is optional).
  - Not in this version: no low flyover of each outing, no year poster (mosaic), no weather per outing.

## 4.1.0 (October 2026)

- **Discreet source credits**: « Habillage › Crédits des sources » now has a choice « Dans le film »: « Discrets » (the default: « © IGN · © Mapterhorn · © OpenStreetMap · Open-Meteo.com » in small capitals, small and translucent, no box, in the corner already chosen), « Complets » (the whole sentence, as before) or « À la fin seulement » (which still keeps Open-Meteo and Esri, whose terms ask for a permanent credit). The full credits always close the film (at the end of the credits roll of the closing card, which then stops with them on screen, or along the bottom edge over the last seconds, above the card), and the poster keeps them in full. The OpenStreetMap credit names the layers drawn (« Repères, eau, forêts et glaciers »). The short form of each source is justified in `docs/sources.md`. Projects saved with the old switch open with « Discrets » (on) or « À la fin seulement » (off). « Copier les crédits » in the export drawer copies the credits with their links, for the description of a video.
- **Timeline ruler**: the ruler now ends with the film. The last time label (« 0:30 ») used to stick out to the right of the closing card and a scrollbar appeared although every block was already in view; the label now sits inside the film's end and the scrollbar only shows when you zoom in.
- **The render history knows the snow and the forests**: « Historique des rendus » now records the snow mode and the forests (on or off, density) of each film, shows them in the details (« — » for a film rendered before) and « Réutiliser ces réglages » puts them back with the rest.
- **The render time counts the forests and the snow**: the « ≈ … de rendu » estimate adjusts the speed of your last film when the forests or the snow differ (about +15 % for forests at 60 trees per hectare, +3 % for snow). It remains an estimate, as the tooltip of the summary says.
- **« Film automatique » offered right after an import**: the message that follows the import of a first track has a « Film automatique… » button that opens the same window as the « Survol » tab, and the home screen says that « Film automatique » makes a finished film in one click. Adding a track to a film shows no such button.
- **A « Forêts » chip in the status bar**: like « Eau », it shows while the trees load. It first says « en attente » while the landmarks and the water, requested before, go through OpenStreetMap's server (one request at a time, to respect it), then « chargement ». If the request fails, the chip says so and its hint explains that the trees are simply missing and tried again at the next track or setting change. It shows only when « Forêts » is on, and « Réglages › Barre d'état › Chargements » can hide it (shown by default, also for existing preferences). The help of « Forêts » now says that the trees can take a few minutes on a busy day of the OpenStreetMap server.
- **Glaciers stay white in summer**: the snow follows the weather of the day, so a glacier in August used to show only the photo's ice. The glaciers mapped by OpenStreetMap are now drawn with the snow look whatever the snow line: white, with crevasses (thin dark lines along the flow), a little bluer in the shade, the very steep faces keeping some rock and a soft edge so the outline is not a hard cut. They follow « Météo › Neige »: drawn with « Selon la météo du jour » and « Manuelle », not with « Aucune ». A « Glaciers » chip in the status bar says whether they have loaded, and the OpenStreetMap credit counts them. Needs an internet connection once per track; older projects open unchanged.
- **The flyover set by its duration or by the marker's speed**: in « Survol › Durée et rythme », « Régler par » switches the « Durée du survol » control between two faces of one setting.
  - « Durée »: the slider now moves one second at a time (15 s to 10 min as before) and the value can be typed in seconds.
  - « Vitesse du marqueur »: a slider of the marker's average speed, in « × temps réel » (the speed-up over the recorded time) and km/h, evenly spread in scale; the value can be typed in either unit. A track without times (a planned outing) has km/h only.
  - Whatever the face, the other one is written beneath (« soit × 240 (sortie de 4 h 00) · 300 km/h », « soit 2 min »). Only the duration is saved: older projects and the « Film automatique » gabarits open unchanged. The speed is the average one: « Garder la durée du survol », the stops, the slow-motion and the speed portions still take their time from the duration. With « À la suite » the length is that of the whole route, and a stage with its own duration keeps it.
- **Render queue**: two small arrows move a waiting project up or down. The still image and the poster ticked in « Plusieurs formats » are queued with the project (the still image where the playback was when it was added). The desktop app keeps the folder of the last run and does not ask again while the app stays open and the folder is there; after a restart, or if the folder is gone, the dialog opens in it. « Changer de dossier » forgets the folder, so the next run asks for a new one from scratch; on the web it is still asked each time. Older queues open unchanged.
- **Rendering in the background no longer keeps a processor core busy**: with the tab hidden or the window minimised, the export waits for tiles on a timer that the browser does not slow down, instead of looping.
- **Frozen lakes**: a lake above the snow line (« Neige », weather of the day or manual) turns into matt white ice, without waves or sun glint, over the same transition as the snow of the ground. Rivers and the sea are not frozen.
- **Streams visible from high up**: the rivers drawn as lines are widened as the camera rises, so a stream stays at least about 1.5 pixels wide on a 1080-pixel-high film (at most 4 times its real width).
- **The three gabarits of « Film automatique » are cards you can read at a glance**: « Reel 30 s », « Résumé 1 min » and « Souvenir 3 min » each show their format (9:16 or 16:9), the length of the film as a big figure and three or four facts with an icon (the camera, the title and closing card, the stops and slow motion, the counters and cards). Choose a card, then « Appliquer ». One sentence says that everything stays editable and that « Annuler » reverts. On a phone the cards stack.
- **Films that look better out of the box**: the closing card of « Souvenir 3 min » has four figures instead of five, the opening title no longer fades out slowly on a long film, and the gabarit now uses the pale cards of the « Application » style, readable over a forest and over snow where the thin letters of the old style were not.
- **Water and forests for every track of the film**: with « À la suite », each stage now has its own water and trees (before, the later stages flew over bare relief), and in a ghost race the second track's surroundings too. Each track asks OpenStreetMap once, one request after the other, so a film of many stages waits longer for its last stage; removing a track removes its water and trees at once.
- **Forests thinned, not cut**: when a long track has more forest than the device can draw, the far forests used to be left out altogether. They are now all there, thinner and thinner with the distance to the track (the nearest track when there are several), so the total stays within the device limit. Older projects open unchanged.

## 4.0.0 (October 2026)

- **A film in one click**: the « Survol » tab starts with « Film automatique ». Three gabarits, « Reel 30 s » (vertical, 30 s), « Résumé 1 min » and « Souvenir 3 min » (16:9), set in one step the length, camera, slow motion and stops, overlay style, opening title and closing card with their figures, day or stage cards and the export format. « Annuler » puts everything back, and everything stays editable. Your own texts and the stops you placed by hand are never replaced. No music is added.
- **A render queue**: in « Exporter › Plusieurs formats », « File de rendus » renders several projects one after the other while you are away.
  - Open a project, tick its formats, « Ajouter ce projet à la file », then open the next one. Each project is copied when added: editing it later does not change its film.
  - « Lancer la file » asks for the folder once, opens each project in turn, renders it, and puts the project that was open back as it was. A failed project is marked and the queue goes on; « Annuler le rendu en cours » stops the run.
  - The queue is kept when the app is closed: « Reprendre » restarts the interrupted film from its first image. A notification tells you when the queue ends if the window is not in front (web only).
- **The render goes on in the background**: with the window minimised or the tab hidden, the export no longer slows down to a crawl while it waits for tiles.

## 0.1.0 (October 2026)

First release: the website and the Windows, macOS and Linux desktop applications, from the same code, 100 % local.

- **Import**: GPX and FIT (in-house decoder; heart rate, cadence, power, temperature), several tracks at once, Strava
  activities through the user's own Strava application; planned outings without times (date, start, pace).
- **Terrain and imagery**: Mapterhorn / AWS Terrain Tiles; IGN and swisstopo orthophotos chosen automatically, Esri
  (optional) and Sentinel-2 elsewhere, topographic maps, historical IGN photos; exaggeration; lakes and rivers from
  OpenStreetMap rendered as reflecting water; offline packs along the track.
- **Flyover**: five camera styles and twelve presets, opening and closing shots including the situation shot
  (place among the areas around the track, hold then push-in, framing, moving sun), camera smoothing in space and in
  film time, pacing with slow-motion and pauses at highlights, stops with their own camera.
- **Light and weather**: physically based sky and haze with the real sun, terrain shadows, starry night; historical
  or forecast weather (Open-Meteo); volumetric clouds and a sea of clouds, volumetric or as a lit surface (« Nappe »).
- **Look**: colour grading presets; lens effects off by default: speed blur, bloom, lens flare, depth of field.
- **Landmarks and roadbook**: summits, passes, huts, lakes from OpenStreetMap; categorised climbs; 3D labels; points
  of interest; roadbook of the outing.
- **Several tracks**: ghost race with leaderboard; stages flown one after the other, each with its own colour, name,
  figures, sun and weather; chaining into a single route.
- **Timeline and overlay**: texts, photos and videos (with their sound) on a timeline, music tracks with fades;
  overlay with titles, counters, profile, mini-map, weather, logo, credits, in three styles.
- **Export**: MP4 or WebM up to 4K and 60 fps in five aspect ratios, still image, overlay alone with transparency,
  several formats in one go, poster (A4, A3, square, 300 dpi); on the desktop, one film per track of a folder and a
  command line, hardware H.264 encoder on Linux (NVENC, VAAPI) with a software fallback.
- **Projects**: project files, undo / redo, presets, « Mes projets » with thumbnails; projects of earlier formats
  still open.
- **Quality**: French interface with tips, error messages in French, error screen, WebGL loss handled; about 1,600
  unit tests, end-to-end tests in a real browser, coverage badge; third-party notices shipped with the installers.

The checks that need a GPU were listed in `docs/tests-gpu.md` (since folded into the developer handbook).
